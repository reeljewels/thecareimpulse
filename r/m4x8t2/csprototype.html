Caregiver Security Check

The Consent Record asks what you agreed to. This asks whether you can hold those limits safely, and what caregiving is costing you.

A caregiver’s right to refuse means little if using it puts them at risk.

1

Before you start

If you need help now, you don’t have to finish this first.
  • Thoughts of hurting yourself, or of being better off not here: call or text 988, any time.
  • Danger right now: call 911.
  • Feeling unsafe at home, or being threatened or hurt by a family member: National Domestic Violence Hotline, 1-800-799-7233.
  • Sexual violence, which this tool doesn’t ask about: National Sexual Assault Hotline, 1-800-656-4673.
All are free, confidential, and open 24/7.

Go at your own pace. Remembering all of this can bring things up, sometimes more than you expect. That’s a normal reaction, not a sign something’s wrong with you. Every question is optional. You can skip anything, stop, or come back another time. If it stirs up a lot, talk to someone you trust, or call or text 988.

This is yours. It isn’t signed by the person you care for, and it isn’t sent anywhere. You decide if anyone else sees it.

Nothing is saved. If you close or refresh this page, your answers are gone. That’s on purpose. In a shared household, saved answers can be found. To keep a copy, print it or save it as a PDF at the end, and store it somewhere only you can open.

Quick exit, at the top of the screen, clears everything and opens a weather page. It can’t erase your browser history or anything you’ve already printed or saved. If someone else uses this device, open this page in a private or incognito window.

Before you share anything you write here: counselors, social workers, and clinicians are often mandated reporters. Some answers could require them to make a report. Ask what they’d have to report before you show them. More on this in section 6.

This isn’t a diagnosis, and it doesn’t label anyone as abusive. It puts what you live with into words, so you can take it to someone who can help, like a caregiver support center, a counselor, or a social worker you trust.

9

Read it back

This is what you reported, in plain language. Read it slowly. Many caregivers have never seen the whole picture in one place, and seeing it can hit hard. If it does, you don’t have to sort it out alone: talk to someone you trust, or call or text 988.

Making a copy to share? You can leave these out of the printed or copied version:
Before you share, ask the person what they’d be required to report.

Working title, private prototype. This hasn’t been validated as a screening instrument. Some questions are adapted from domestic-violence screening (the HITS screener) and from research on coercive control. Others come from caregiving experience. By design, it doesn’t ask about sexual violence; that needs referral pathways a reflection tool can’t promise. Before any real use, it needs review by clinicians, caregiver-support professionals, and a lawyer, and a check against existing validated tools.
