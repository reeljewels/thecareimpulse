Caregiver Scope and Consent Record

Put in writing what you can help with, what you can’t, and for how long, before care becomes an assumption.

For unpaid family and informal caregivers whose responsibilities are often assumed rather than agreed to, so a labor of love doesn’t become extraction.

A patient may name a preferred caregiver. Only the caregiver can agree to the work.

A beta tool from The Care Impulse by Ginny L. Sayre

Nothing you enter leaves this device. There’s no submit button. You choose whether to print it, save it, or send it.

Share it with the people who plan care: a discharge planner, case manager, social worker, the patient’s doctors, or your family. Bring it to a care conference or a meeting with a social worker. It works best as the start of a conversation, not a replacement for one. Afterward, sending it in writing creates a record of what was discussed.

Uncovered care

Anything you declined or haven’t been trained for still needs a plan. Arranging that plan is the care team’s job, not something that falls back on you by default.

These are roles for others to confirm, not yours. If you don’t know, leave them blank, and the record will ask the care team or family to name someone.

5

Review and send

This is the record the care team will see. Download or print it, then send the email below with it attached. Using someone else’s device? Download your record, then choose Start over so your answers aren’t left behind.

If setting limits could put your housing, finances, or safety at risk, think carefully about timing and about who sees this record. A social worker or a caregiver support organization can help you plan how and when to share it.

Email to the care team

Sending this creates a dated record that the team received your limits. Ask that it be added to the patient’s chart. Many hospitals prefer messages through the patient portal, so you can paste it there instead. For long records, use Copy email rather than Open in email app, since some email apps cut long links short.

Open in email app

This beta tool provides general information and is not legal or medical advice. The record it creates is a communication tool, not a legal document. Anything you share with a care team can become part of the medical record and may be reviewed later, including in legal proceedings. Describe your limits; you don’t need to share diagnoses. It doesn’t appoint a medical decision-maker, create employment, or waive any rights. Declining future tasks doesn’t erase care you’ve already provided.
