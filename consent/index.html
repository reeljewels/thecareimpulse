Caregiver Scope and Consent Record

Put in writing what you agree to do, what you don’t, and for how long, before care becomes an assumption.

A patient may name a preferred caregiver. Only the caregiver can agree to the work.

A beta tool from The Care Impulse by Ginny L. Sayre

1

You and this record

Everything you enter stays on this device. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you send it. Your answers are saved in this browser so you can come back to them. On a shared or hospital computer, download your record when you’re done, then use Start over to clear it.

Where is this care being planned?

Agreement doesn’t renew on its own.

2

Task by task

Answer only the tasks the care plan expects from you. Skip the rest, or mark them “Doesn’t apply.”

3

Time and availability

Agreeing to a task isn’t agreeing to unlimited availability. Travel, waiting, phone calls, and portal work count toward these limits.

4

Uncovered care

Anything you declined or haven’t been trained for still needs a plan. Arranging that plan is the care team’s job, not something that falls back on you by default.

5

Review and send

This is the record the care team will see. Download or print it, then send the email below with it attached. Using someone else’s device? Download your record, then choose Start over so your answers aren’t left behind.

Email to the care team

Sending this creates a dated record that the team received your limits. Ask that it be added to the patient’s chart.

Open in email app

This beta tool provides general information and is not legal or medical advice. The record it creates is a communication tool, not a legal document. It doesn’t appoint a medical decision-maker, create employment, or waive any rights. Declining future tasks doesn’t erase care you’ve already provided.
