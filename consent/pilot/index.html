Caregiver Scope and Consent Record · Caregiver review

What happened after you shared it?

If you used the Caregiver Scope and Consent Record, a few short answers can show whether it does what it’s meant to: help caregivers say what they can and can’t take on, and help care teams see tasks nobody has agreed to cover.

Joining is optional. You already have everything the form offers. Saying no to this changes nothing.

Your record is not sent to us. Nothing you entered in the form leaves your device. These questions ask only what happened afterward. Please don’t include names, hospitals, or medical details in your answers.

The questions

Answer whenever you’re ready. A few weeks after you share your record is a good time. “Not yet” and “I don’t know” are real answers, so you never need to chase down a chart to respond.

  1. Did you share your record? With a nurse or case manager · a doctor · family · someone else · not yet
  2. Did anyone on the care team discuss it with you? Yes · briefly · no · not yet
  3. As far as you know, was it added to the chart? Yes · no · they declined · I don’t know
  4. Did anything change about who was expected to do what? A task was reassigned · a referral or service was arranged · a gap was named but not filled · nothing changed · not yet · I don’t know
  5. Anything else you want us to know? Optional

How answers are used

Answers are anonymous: the questionnaire does not ask for your name or email, and it isn’t linked to your record. Results may be summarized to improve the form and to show care teams and policymakers whether it helps, never with anything that identifies you.

This is offered as part of a review with partner organizations. If you were invited by one, they may send you a single reminder, and they can tell you more about the review.

Agreeing to answer these questions is not agreement to anything else.
